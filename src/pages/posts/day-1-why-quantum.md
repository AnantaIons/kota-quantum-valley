Today I'm starting something new: learning quantum computing from zero, and writing down every step.

## Why me?

I write embedded firmware for a living — bare-metal C, microcontrollers, real hardware. That turns out to matter. A quantum computer isn't just physics and maths. Someone has to build the electronics and firmware that **control** the qubits: sending precise pulses, reading tiny signals, and doing it all in perfect timing.

That's an engineering problem. And it's one I already understand from the other side.

## Why in public?

Two reasons:

1. **Writing forces clarity.** If I can't explain a concept simply, I haven't understood it yet.
2. **Kota students deserve a head start.** If I can learn this, so can someone who has just finished JEE physics — and these notes will be free.

## The plan

> Math base → core concepts → first circuits on real hardware → algorithms → quantum control hardware.

I'll post every few days. Each post covers one thing I learned, what confused me, and what finally made it click.
